When a marriage has broken down, the decision to begin divorce proceedings can feel urgent. Taking time to organise the relevant information first can make the initial legal consultation more focused and help you understand the questions that may arise.

## Identify the type of marriage

The first step is to establish how the marriage was celebrated and registered. Kenya recognises several systems of marriage, and the documents and legal questions involved may differ. If available, locate the marriage certificate or other records relating to the marriage.

## Gather the core documents

It is helpful to collect copies of the records that are relevant to your family and financial circumstances. These may include:

- Identification documents and the marriage certificate
- Birth certificates for any children
- Relevant correspondence between the spouses
- Records relating to school fees, healthcare and other child-related costs
- Title documents, sale agreements or loan records for significant property
- Bank, business or employment records where financial questions may arise

Keep the original records safe. A lawyer can advise which documents are relevant to your particular circumstances.

## Consider arrangements for children

Where children are involved, their welfare is central. Think carefully about their present living arrangements, schooling, healthcare, day-to-day care and financial support. Avoid making assumptions about the final legal outcome before receiving advice based on the specific facts.

## Separate the property questions

Divorce and the division of matrimonial property involve related but distinct legal questions. Prepare a clear list of property acquired before and during the marriage, how it was acquired, how it is registered and the contributions each spouse made. Contributions may require a careful assessment and should not be reduced to title documents alone.

## Preserve the record

Do not delete messages, financial records or other information that could later be relevant. Keep a chronological note of important events while they are still fresh in your memory. Avoid unlawfully accessing another person's private accounts or devices.

## Get advice before taking irreversible steps

Moving children, disposing of property, emptying accounts or publishing private allegations may create further legal and practical difficulties. Early advice can help you understand the available process, likely timelines and immediate safeguards without committing you to filing a case that day.

Every family situation is different. A confidential consultation should address the type of marriage, the circumstances leading to separation, any children, property and any immediate safety concerns.
